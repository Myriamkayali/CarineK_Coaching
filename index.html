The Word Swap Method™

Catch the word. Name it. Swap it.

Most of what keeps you stuck isn’t a lack of effort. It’s a word. The one you reach for automatically when things get hard quietly decides how you feel, whether you try again, and how fast you move on.

1

Catch it

Notice the sentence the moment it happens. Not later, in hindsight. While it’s still live in your head.

2

Name the word

Find the one word doing the damage: always, never, failure, can’t. It’s usually smaller than you’d think.

3

Swap it

Replace it with a word that’s still honest, but forward-looking instead of final. Not a forced positive, just a door left open.

Before

“I always mess this up.”

Name the word

“Always” isn’t accurate, and it closes the door on change.

Swap

“I’ve messed this up before, and I’m still learning what works.”

Time to believe it: about two weeks of repeating the swap before it felt true.

Psychologists call this cognitive reframing. Decades of research back it. The Word Swap Method just makes it small enough to use mid-moment.

This method works, but on its own it’s not always enough. Underneath the one word are patterns you’ve been running for a long time, barriers that show up in the same spots, on repeat, in ways that are hard to see from the inside. Catching a word is a skill. Building a new script for those patterns, one you can actually use under pressure, is coaching work.
